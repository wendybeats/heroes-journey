# Art direction — reference briefing, 2026-09-28

These are user-specified directions. The first character image is a concept for review, not approval of final art or a production sprite sheet.

## Color and backdrops

- Use highly stylized color combinations, leaning slightly muted without becoming washed out.
- Backdrops use two or three shades at most. They support the central character and must fit within a clean interface.
- Fade or dither/pixelate scene edges so they transition into a monochrome black or white app background without a hard rectangular boundary.
- Keep background scenes separate from character assets. Transparency will allow the scene's edge treatment to work with either interface background.
- Reference boards 1 and 2 establish color relationships and atmosphere, not a requirement to copy their visual density or broad palettes.

## Characters

- Simplified pixel characters with near-full-height human proportions, like the standing pixel-character lineup in reference board 4.
- Older Pokémon is a reference for economical animation and pixel readability, while body proportions should be taller than its small overworld characters.
- Grimdark mood with selective strong light contrast. Keep the base character dark and restrained; special equipment such as gloves or glasses can carry maximum contrast.
- Avoid excessive brightness, many unrelated colors, and elaborate detail.
- Starter clothing: a simple futuristic bodysuit.
- First requested concept: male character with black hair.

## First concept assumptions

Use a neutral idle-ready standing pose, a slight three-quarter turn, short black hair, muted charcoal/slate clothing, and a medium warm skin tone. These are reversible choices for the first image, not new locked requirements. Produce a transparent character cutout to judge independently of the backdrop.

The initial concept is a flattened still image. A consistent production pixel grid, palette, layers, attachment points and animated frames must be validated during the later production pilot. The generator's enlarged pixel appearance alone does not prove those properties.
